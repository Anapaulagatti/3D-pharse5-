# 2. DEFINE JUNI-LEVEL ARTISTIC QUALITY

Juni is the approved visual benchmark.

The new NPCs should belong to the same fictional world and art direction.

Match the approved characters in:

## Character proportions

- Chibi body construction.
- Head-to-body ratio.
- Shoulder and torso size.
- Arm and leg proportions.
- Hand and footwear scale.
- Native foot-ground relationship.
- Overall character scale in Phaser.

## Facial quality

- Expressive eyes.
- Defined eyelids and eyebrows.
- Carefully positioned highlights.
- Attractive and distinctive hairstyles.
- Subtle cheek and face shading.
- Natural mouth expressions.
- Individually designed character identities.

NPCs should have faces as carefully illustrated as the playable characters.

## Clothing quality

- Properly constructed Korean clothing.
- Visible collars, cuffs and seams.
- Appropriate garment folds.
- Clear fabric overlap.
- Distinctive footwear.
- Carefully placed accessories.
- Consistent lighting.
- Readable garment depth.

Traditional Korean garments should have coherent Korean-inspired construction rather than generic geometric robes.

## Pixel-art craftsmanship

- Rich but controlled pixel detailing.
- Purposeful pixel clusters.
- Consistent outline treatment.
- Coherent highlights and shadows.
- Stable material identity.
- No random dithering.
- No blurred pixel boundaries.
- No unintended antialiasing.
- No jagged extraction artifacts.
- No disconnected hair or clothing pieces.

Match the approved roster's visual sophistication rather than merely matching the atlas dimensions.

# 5. SOURCE ARTWORK BEFORE ANIMATION

For each pilot:

## Step A — Identity master

Create high-quality original full-body source artwork.

Match Juni's approved visual direction.

Preserve individual facial identity.

Do not directly produce 104 low-quality frames from one mathematical body model.

## Step B — Four independent views

Produce and refine:

- Front.
- Back.
- Left.
- Right.

Views must maintain the same face, hairstyle, costume, proportions, accessories and palette.

Side views must genuinely represent the character rather than mirroring an incorrectly drawn front view.

## Step C — Native pixel finishing

Convert to the approved native art format using a deliberate, controlled finishing process.

Use:

- 96×112 frame registration.
- Foot anchor (48,100).
- Existing nearest-neighbor rendering.
- Approved native player scale.

The high-resolution source is not itself the final native pixel art.

Do not rely solely on an automatic resize to achieve the required craftsmanship.

Inspect and correct eyes, hair, fingers, garment edges and footwear at native scale.

## Step D — Animation rig

Use appropriately authored body/limb layers and Juni-referenced motion phases.

Preserve the pilot's own source artwork.

Retarget the motion rather than drawing every limb as a plain polygon.

## Step E — Animation export

Maintain the current compatible NPC clip contract:

- Idle: 2 frames × 4 directions.
- Walk: 8 frames × 4 directions.
- Greet: 8 frames × 4 directions.
- Activity: 8 frames × 4 directions.

Total: 104 native cells per NPC.

Animation must preserve source-art quality during:

- Front walking.
- Back walking.
- Left walking.
- Right walking.
- Greeting.
- Occupational activity.

Do not reproduce the previously rejected front/back gait problems or backward-looking right walk.
