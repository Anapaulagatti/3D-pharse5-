## Original Korean Arena design conclusions

These are our interpretations, not claims that Pixels uses these exact rules:

1. A social center needs useful edges: shop fronts, shade, seating and future conversation anchors. The center should not be a single empty tiled rectangle.
2. A hierarchy of consistent walking widths gives directions without excessive labels:112px civic route,80px neighborhood streets,56–64px scenic paths. Juni remains55px native body height.
3. Distinguish public movement, forecourt, building mass and planted/private back edge. Entrances face a route; landscaping never repairs an incoherent street diagram.
4. Make loops useful: civic shopping circuit, a separated station crossing, a landscaped Namsan loop, an enclosed leisure campus and a Jeju scenic circuit. Optional branches end at actual viewpoints or activities.
5. Establish differences in materials, boundaries and spatial character: contemporary Korean civic paving, steel/glass transit, colorful cultural leisure, forest trails and basalt island paths.
6. Put the new Namsan in the northeast, rail district southwest, leisure southeast and island southwest. These placements are fresh design decisions from the protected north interface and native artwork dimensions, not inherited rejected southern coordinates.
7. Keep the ferry the sole mainland/island transport connection. One boarding sequence is clearer than a contradictory decorative bridge.

This fictional Vietnamese-first Korean learning town adopts none of Pixels' proprietary artwork, map geometry, branding, farming economy, tokens or NFT systems. Future learning/social activities are reserved as anchors, not added as unrelated systems in this environment pass.
